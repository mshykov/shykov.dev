# Lisbon vs Madrid for an Engineering Manager

I lived in Lisbon for two years, then in Madrid for two years, and now I'm moving back to Lisbon.
People ask me which city is better. For daily life the answer is personal. For the job market I
wanted numbers, so I collected every engineering-management job post published in both cities
in one month and read them.

This is the comparison from one specific seat: an engineering manager who works in English and
doesn't speak fluent Spanish or Portuguese.

## In short

- **The markets are closer in size than the cities.** In one month I found 18 software
  engineering-management roles tied to Lisbon and 24 tied to Madrid. Greater Madrid has more than
  twice the population of Greater Lisbon.
- **Language is the real difference.** None of the 18 Lisbon roles requires Portuguese. 5 of the
  24 Madrid roles are written in Spanish or ask for it, often at a native level. That leaves 19
  Madrid roles open to an English-only manager, against 18 in Lisbon: twice the people, just as
  many jobs in English.
- **Lisbon is a city of international engineering hubs.** 15 of the 18 Lisbon roles are at
  companies headquartered abroad. In Madrid a third of the roles are at Spanish companies.
- **Lisbon shows salaries more often.** 6 of 18 Lisbon posts state a range, against 2 of 24 in
  Madrid. Inside Lisbon an international hub pays about twice what a local consultancy offers.
- **Daily life is a real trade.** Madrid gives you more flat for the money, better transport and
  faster public services. Lisbon gives you the ocean, English everywhere, kind people and lower
  everyday prices.

## How I counted

I searched LinkedIn job posts published in the last 30 days in Greater Lisbon and Greater Madrid
for Engineering Manager, Head, Director and VP of Engineering, Software Development Manager and
Engineering Lead. Then I read every post and removed two kinds:

| | Lisbon | Madrid |
|---|---|---|
| Posts found | 30 | 39 |
| Not software (construction, energy, hotels, pharma projects) | 5 | 11 |
| Not tied to the city (fully remote, agencies, talent pools) | 7 | 4 |
| **Software engineering leadership in the city** | **18** | **24** |

The word "engineering" does a lot of work in Madrid: more than a quarter of the posts were for
water treatment, power plants, rail projects or a hotel.

To check that LinkedIn wasn't hiding a local market, I also searched the largest local tech job
boards: Tecnoempleo in Spain and ITJobs.pt in Portugal. More on what I found there below.

## How many roles

18 in Lisbon, 24 in Madrid. Madrid has a third more roles, from a metropolitan area of about 7
million people against about 3 million around Lisbon. Per person, Lisbon has more
engineering-management roles than Madrid.

Both numbers are small. A month with 18 relevant roles means the search is about the right few
companies, not about volume.

## Who is hiring

| | Lisbon | Madrid |
|---|---|---|
| Headquartered abroad | 15 of 18 | 16 of 24 |
| Headquartered in the same country | 3 of 18 | 8 of 24 |

Lisbon's list reads like a directory of international engineering hubs: Cloudflare with three
open roles, Discord hiring a Director and Site Lead and an Engineering Manager, Datadog, Zendesk,
GoCardless and SIXT. These companies run their Lisbon offices in English.

Madrid has hubs too: Ebury, Fever, Roche, NielsenIQ, Prima, AWS, IKEA and Suntory, among others.
It also has a stronger layer of Spanish companies: Santander, Indra, Civitatis and several
consultancies. That layer is where Spanish comes in.

## Language

This is the number that matters most if you work in English.

| | Lisbon | Madrid |
|---|---|---|
| Post written in the local language | 2 of 18 | 5 of 24 |
| Local language required | **0 of 18** | **5 of 24** |

The two Lisbon posts written in Portuguese ask only for fluent English. The strongest local
language request in Lisbon was "Portuguese strongly preferred" for Discord's Director role.

In Madrid, one post asks for native Spanish with English at B1. A Santander post requires
Spanish. For an English-only engineering manager, the Madrid market is about a fifth smaller
than it looks: 19 roles, one more than Lisbon.

Lisbon has its own language twist: French. A large nearshore sector serves French clients, and
many lead and manager roles there ask for fluent French.

## Salaries

Lisbon posts state a salary range four times as often: 6 of 18 (a third), against 2 of 24 in
Madrid (one in twelve).

| Role | Range (gross per year) |
|---|---|
| Lisbon, Cloudflare, Engineering Manager | EUR 74,000 – 111,000 |
| Lisbon, Cloudflare, Senior Engineering Manager | EUR 89,000 – 122,000 |
| Lisbon, GoCardless, Engineering Manager II | EUR 70,400 – 105,600 |
| Lisbon, local consultancy, Engineering Manager | EUR 35,000 – 42,000 |
| Madrid, NielsenIQ, Engineering Manager | EUR 60,000 – 70,000 |
| Madrid, Prima, Engineering Manager | EUR 70,000 – 90,000 |

Six data points don't make a salary survey. The one clear pattern: inside Lisbon, the same title
pays about twice as much at an international hub as at a local consultancy.

## The local job boards

If the engineering-manager market lived on local boards, LinkedIn would miss it. It doesn't.

- **ITJobs.pt, Lisbon:** ten searches, 70 posts, and not one titled Engineering Manager. Local
  leadership roles are Tech Lead, Team Lead and Project Manager, mostly at consultancies.
- **Tecnoempleo, all of Spain:** nine searches including Spanish titles, 307 posts, about ten at
  engineering-manager level for the whole country, most of them placed by one recruitment agency.
  Two were in Madrid. The local titles are "Jefe de Proyecto", "Tech Lead" and "Responsable
  técnico".

In both countries, the engineering manager is an international-company role. You find it on
LinkedIn and on company career pages. That makes the number of international hubs in a city,
and the language they work in, the thing to compare.

## Read the relocation line

Three Madrid posts set limits that matter to anyone moving in: the AWS role is open to EU
nationals only, IKEA offers no relocation support, and Suntory says immigration support is not
guaranteed. If you need a work permit, read that line before you read the tech stack.

## Life: two years in each city

The job market is half of the decision. Here is the other half, from my own two years in each.

### What Madrid does better

- **Rent for the money.** About EUR 1,600 a month rented me a one-bedroom flat in a new building
  near Atocha, inside the M-30 ring, with a gym, a pool, a concierge, a patio, a terrace and a
  coworking room. In Lisbon I never saw a building with that kind of service, and EUR 1,600
  usually means a plain flat.
- **Getting around without a car.** Good public transport, a flat city you can walk for hours,
  wide pavements and clean streets. Inside the M-30 there is almost no free street parking: blue
  and green zones are paid and time-limited, so you can't drive in and leave the car for a
  working day. Add low-emission rules, and the car stops being the default. Car sharing fills the
  gap, and I used it a lot. And no rental e-scooters on the pavements.
- **Green in the middle of a dry plateau.** The Retiro and a lot of trees for a city that sits in
  semi-arid country.
- **A city that feels prosperous.** Salamanca and Goya are lovely for a slow walk past shop
  windows. People do sport everywhere; there is a small gym on almost every block.
- **Cheaper cars and fuel**, and public services that work faster than in Portugal.

### What Madrid does worse

- **No water.** No ocean, no sea. For me this is the biggest minus, and you feel it every
  weekend.
- **Spanish from day one.** At the doctor, the landlord, the bank, the bar: almost nobody
  switches to English. Start learning Spanish before you move.
- **The summer.** Above 40°C for weeks.

### What Lisbon does better

- **English is not a problem.** Not on the street, not in a government office. You can live in
  English while you learn Portuguese.
- **People.** Portuguese people are kind and welcoming, and you feel it in small daily things.
- **Safety.** Portugal is one of the safest countries in Europe.
- **Nature.** No snow in winter. Summers get hot, but lighter than in Madrid. And the ocean: cold,
  yes, but having that much water next to the city is the biggest plus of all for me.
- **Everyday prices.** Cafés, restaurants and services cost less than in Madrid. Housing is the
  exception.
- **Food.** Very good cooking, and a lot of fish.

### What Lisbon does worse

- **Housing prices.** Rent is high for what you get, as the Madrid flat above shows.
- **A damp winter.** It is short and often sunny, but the damp gets indoors. Prepare for it:
  a dehumidifier and a flat with decent heating.
- **Public services.** Everything is done in person, the queues are long, and things move slowly.

If you have your own flat and rarely deal with government offices, everything else in Lisbon is
very nice.

## Why I'm going back to Lisbon

The ocean and the climate are part of it. The bigger reason is simpler: my son lives in Lisbon,
and for two years I travelled there every month to see him. My role in Madrid ended in
September. And for the job I want, a Head of Engineering role working in English, Lisbon's market
is the better fit: almost as many international hubs as a city twice its size, and none of them
asks for Portuguese. I wrote about the search itself in
[Job Search as an Engineering Manager: My First Month in Numbers](/blog/engineering-manager-job-search-first-month).

## What I'd tell an engineering manager choosing between them

1. **Count the roles you can actually apply for, not the city's size.** Filter by language first.
2. **List the international hubs.** In both cities they are most of the market for this role.
3. **Read the relocation line** if you need a work permit or relocation help.
4. **Expect a wide salary spread in Lisbon.** The same title can pay twice as much at a hub.
5. **Visit Madrid in July and Lisbon in January.** You'll learn more from one week of each than
   from any ranking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need Spanish to work as an engineering manager in Madrid?

Not for every role, but for a meaningful share. In my sample, 5 of 24 Madrid engineering-manager
roles were written in Spanish or required it, some at a native level. International companies in
Madrid usually work in English. Outside work, you need Spanish from the first week.

### Do you need Portuguese to work as an engineering manager in Lisbon?

In my sample, no. None of the 18 Lisbon roles required Portuguese; one Director role said it was
strongly preferred. Several lead roles at nearshore companies required French instead.

### Which city has more engineering manager jobs?

Madrid, but not by much: 24 against 18 in one month, from a metropolitan area more than twice as
large.

### How much does an engineering manager earn in Lisbon?

The ranges published in my sample went from EUR 35,000 – 42,000 at a local consultancy to EUR
70,000 – 122,000 at international engineering hubs such as Cloudflare and GoCardless.

### Is Lisbon or Madrid better for English speakers?

Lisbon. At work both cities have English-speaking hubs, but in Lisbon English also works on the
street and in government offices. In Madrid you need Spanish for daily life from the start.

### Is rent cheaper in Madrid or Lisbon?

In my experience, Madrid gives you more for the same money: around EUR 1,600 a month rented a new
one-bedroom with a gym, pool and concierge inside the M-30. In Lisbon the same budget usually
rents a plain flat. Everyday costs like cafés and services are lower in Lisbon.

## Method and limits

LinkedIn public job search, posts from the last 30 days as of 2 November 2026, Greater Lisbon and
Greater Madrid, eight title searches; every post read and classified by hand. Second check:
ITJobs.pt (Lisbon) and Tecnoempleo (Spain). Limits: one month, small numbers (18 and 24), my own
classification, and LinkedIn sees international companies better than local ones. The life
section is my experience, not data.
